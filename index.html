About myself

I like being the beginner in the room.

“If you want something you've never had, you've got to do something you've never done.”

My interest in business started before I could fully explain it. Around age 6, I was already learning pieces of my family's retail business: how a cash register worked, how sales happened, and why inventory mattered. As I got older, the questions changed from what something cost to why it was priced that way, why customers chose one product over another, and how a business actually earns trust and revenue.

That curiosity became more real when I started helping with the financial side of the business: deposits, reconciliations, inventory orders, customer behavior, and product decisions across 8 high-volume retail locations. Seeing a business at a $50M+ revenue scale made finance feel less abstract. The numbers were not just a class topic. They were tied to real stores, real customers, and real operating decisions.

I like being around people who know more than me. Working with my dad, who has 25+ years of industry experience, showed me how much judgment sits behind pricing, relationships, sourcing, and finding investment opportunities. I knew I was not going to walk into finance knowing everything. That is what made it interesting.

That learning curve is what pulled me toward investment banking. I like the idea of moving across companies, industries, and deal types, then having to learn the details quickly enough to ask better questions. IBE, BCI, IBP, BPEVC, VIRA, and trading competitions have all put me back in beginner mode. From a Micron stock pitch to an IBP sell-side case, from placing 213th globally in IMC Prosperity to helping VIRA win the JPMorgan Chase startup pitch, I keep choosing rooms where I have to learn fast, defend the logic, and solve problems that do not have one right answer.
